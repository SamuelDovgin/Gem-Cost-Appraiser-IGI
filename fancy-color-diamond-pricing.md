## How Fancy Color Lab-Grown Pricing Works

### Key Differences vs White Lab-Grown

1. **Color intensity grade matters enormously.** IGI grades fancy color diamonds on a separate scale: Faint → Very Light → Light → Fancy Light → Fancy → Fancy Intense → Fancy Vivid / Fancy Deep / Fancy Dark. "Fancy Vivid" is always the most valuable grade. A Fancy Vivid stone can be 3–10× more expensive than a Fancy (base) stone of the same carat weight and cut.

2. **Factory supply is less uniform.** Unlike white diamonds (grown to order in massive batches), fancy color production is more variable. A Fancy Vivid Green at 3ct is genuinely harder to grow than a Fancy Yellow at 3ct. Supply depth matters.

3. **Shape is less penalized for color stones.** Cushion, radiant, and pear cuts are preferred for fancy colors because they concentrate color saturation. Round brilliants are less common and often command a small premium for color stones.

4. **IGI coverage varies by color.** Pink, yellow, and blue have abundant IGI-certified supply. Green, orange, and red are sparser — many listings show GRC, SGL, or other certifications. Always verify the cert lab. IGI international is the standard used in this research.

5. **Clarity matters less than for white.** Color dominates the visual appearance. VS2 and even SI1 are acceptable for most fancy colors because the color masks inclusions. VVS+ commands a modest premium for color stones vs a steep one for whites.

### 5. RED

**IGI grade range sold:** Fancy Purplish Red → Fancy Red → Fancy Vivid Red  
**Growth method:** HPHT or CVD; red is produced by plastic deformation of the crystal lattice. Very difficult to achieve even in lab conditions.  
**Demand (2026):** ★★★☆☆ — Niche but growing. True IGI "Fancy Red" (no modifier) is the rarest of all diamond color grades, even in lab-grown. Most "red" stones on Alibaba are actually Fancy Purplish Red or Fancy Brownish Red.

### 8. BLACK

**Certification:** Black diamonds are typically not graded on the standard D–Z or fancy color scale. IGI grades them as "Fancy Black" (no clarity grade possible on opaque stones). Many black diamonds come with simple authentication cards, not full IGI reports.

**Demand (2026):** ★★★☆☆ — Steady niche demand, especially for men's rings and gothic/alternative jewelry. High supply.

| Black lab-grown with IGI "Fancy Black" cert | ~$40–$100/ct | Premium for authenticated cert; rare for black |

**Notes:**
- Black diamonds don't follow the standard clarity/color premium model. Price is almost entirely driven by size and polish quality.
- "Natural" black diamonds (carbonado) sell for more than lab black but are a completely different product. Most retail black diamonds sold in the last 5 years are lab-grown or irradiation-treated.
- Black diamond price is essentially flat per carat — there's no meaningful VVS1-vs-VS1 premium because you can't grade clarity in an opaque stone.
- For appraisal context: a 3ct black lab-grown solitaire ring stone likely cost the jeweler $50–$120 for the stone alone regardless of clarity claim.

### 9. BROWN / CHAMPAGNE / COGNAC

**IGI grade range:** Fancy Light Brown → Fancy Brown → Fancy Brownish → Fancy Dark Brown  
**Growth method:** CVD or HPHT — brown is a natural byproduct of nitrogen or structural defects. Not intentionally produced; often the result of imperfect CVD growth.  
**Demand (2026):** ★★★☆☆ — Steady and growing. "Champagne" and "cognac" marketing has helped normalize brown stones. Used heavily in fashion/boho jewelry.

#### Wholesale Price Anchors (brown/champagne lab-grown)

| Type | Price Signal | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Fancy Brownish Yellow / Champagne general | $168–$199 per 10ct minimum | Per-carat ~$17–$20/ct at bulk minimum; very cheap |
| Fancy Brown / Champagne (single stone, IGI) | ~$40–$80/ct | Retail-facing single stone pricing |
| Fancy Dark Brown | ~$25–$50/ct | Low premium; near-commodity |
| "Cognac" (Fancy Brownish Orange) | ~$80–$150/ct | Marketing premium on Brownish Orange |

**Notes:**
- Brown and champagne lab-grown diamonds are among the cheapest fancy colors — often only a small step above heavily tinted S–Z white diamonds (which the existing research prices at $0.32/ct × G baseline = extremely cheap).
- "Champagne" is a marketing term. Always look at the actual IGI color grade. "Fancy Yellow Brown" ≠ "Fancy Brown Yellow" — the dominant color comes last in IGI grading.
- "Cognac" stones are Fancy Brownish Orange or Fancy Orangey Brown — slightly more valuable than plain brown but still a modest premium.
- For appraisal context: if a customer's stone is brown/champagne and NOT set in high-demand designer jewelry, the stone cost is low regardless of size. A 2ct champagne lab-grown costs the jeweler ~$60–$160 total.

### 10. CHAMELEON

**Description:** Chameleon diamonds temporarily change color (from greenish yellow to yellow) when heated or kept in the dark. Extremely rare even in lab-grown production.  
**IGI grade:** "Fancy Dark Gray-Yellowish Green" when stable, temporarily changes to "Fancy Brownish Yellow" when heated. IGI notes the color-change phenomenon on the cert.  
**Demand (2026):** ★★☆☆☆ — Highly niche collector market. Almost no reliable Alibaba data.

#### Estimated Pricing (chameleon lab-grown)

| Type | Estimated $/ct | Confidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chameleon (lab-grown, IGI verified) | ~$400–$1,000/ct for 1–3ct | Very low | Thin market; no reliable direct comps |

**Notes:**
- Chameleon lab-grown diamonds are so rare on the Alibaba market that there is essentially no meaningful price ladder.
- If a customer presents a chameleon diamond, the most important step is verifying the IGI certificate documents the color-change behavior. Without that, a "chameleon" claim is unverifiable.
- Retail pricing for chameleon diamonds is largely driven by novelty and storytelling, not a tight market comp.

## Clarity Adjustment for Fancy Color Stones

Unlike white diamonds, fancy color stones tolerate lower clarity grades because color masks inclusions.

| Clarity | Multiplier vs VS1 baseline (color stones) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| IF | 1.10–1.20× | Minimal premium vs white's steep IF premium |
| VVS1 | 1.05–1.12× | Small bump; color dominates value |
| VVS2 | 1.02–1.06× | Negligible vs VS1 |
| VS1 | 1.00× | **Baseline for color stones** |
| VS2 | 0.93–0.97× | Slight discount; usually still eye-clean |
| SI1 | 0.85–0.92× | Eye-clean possible depending on cut; popular price tier |
| SI2 | 0.72–0.82× | Inclusions may show in large or step cuts; acceptable for brilliant cuts |
| I1 | 0.55–0.70× | Visible inclusions; significant discount |

**Rule:** For fancy color appraisal, a VS2 color stone is ~5% less than VS1 (vs ~7–10% for white). SI1 is common and acceptable for brilliant cuts.

## What the App Should Do with Color Stones

1. **Identify color first, then grade.** The color grade (Fancy Vivid Pink vs Fancy Pink) is the single most impactful variable — far more than clarity or cut for color stones.

2. **Use the multiplier table above** to convert from the white G/VS1 baseline wholesale cost to an estimated color stone jeweler cost.

3. **Apply a narrower clarity adjustment** than for white stones (the table above).

4. **Flag if the color is rare.** Red, orange, purple, and chameleon have very thin market data. Flag these as "specialty stone — limited comp data" and recommend a colored diamond specialist appraisal.

5. **Ask for the IGI cert for color.** The exact color description on the IGI cert (e.g., "Fancy Vivid Purplish Pink" vs "Fancy Vivid Pink") has a large price impact. Always use the cert language, not the seller's marketing name.

6. **Note that black and brown/champagne are commodity-priced.** These don't follow the escalating color scale — they're near-commodity and should be priced accordingly.
