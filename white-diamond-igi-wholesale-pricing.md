| 5.00 ct | 1.49× (+49%) | 1.48× (+48%) |

**Why G-color premiums are less extreme than D at 2–3ct:** D+VVS1 at 3ct is a rarity multiplier effect (two hard-to-achieve grading factors combine). G/VVS1 at 3ct is more achievable, so the market premium is more moderate. However, at 5ct+ even G/VVS1 is genuinely hard to produce, so premiums converge.

**Rule:** A flat clarity multiplier (same % regardless of carat weight) is wrong. At 3ct, VVS1 costs ~32% more than VS1 (G color). At 10ct, VVS1 costs ~75% more than VS1. At 1ct the difference is only ~9%.

### Clarity Scaling Rules

1. **Under 1ct:** Clarity premiums compress. A 0.5ct VVS1 is only ~8% more than a 0.5ct VS1 at jeweler cost. Very little reason to buy IF at sub-1ct.
2. **1–2ct sweet zone:** Standard multipliers apply. VVS1 is ~9–16% over VS1.  
3. **3–5ct critical zone:** VVS1 is 32–48% over VS1. IF is 38–55% over VS1. This is where clarity grade matters enormously to cost.
4. **6ct+ rarity zone:** IF and VVS1 at 6ct+ are rarely stocked. Expect wide price variance and long lead times. VVS1 is 55–75% over VS1 here.
5. **SI1 and SI2 at large sizes:** Heavily discounted. A 5ct SI2 round lab-grown is hard to resell — inclusions visible under the open table. Expect 45–50% below VS1 cost at 5ct+.

## Section 8: Price Rules Summary

### Rule 1: The Carat Non-Linearity
Lab-grown diamond price per carat is **NOT linear**. It falls at 2.5–3ct (oversupply pressure on the most common sizes) and then rises again at 4ct+ as production complexity increases. A 4ct stone is not simply 4× the 1ct price.

### Rule 2: Clarity Scales Hard at Large Sizes
The single biggest mistake in pricing is applying a flat clarity multiplier. A VVS1 at 1ct is only ~9% more than VS1. At 5ct it is ~48% more. At 10ct it is ~75% more. **Budget VVS1 is expensive at large sizes.**

### Rule 3: Color Premium is Compressed in Lab-Grown
Unlike natural diamonds where D can be 3–5× the price of K at 2ct+, lab-grown D is only ~12% over G. The factories grow high-color stones routinely. This means H or I color is a very practical buying choice without major cost penalty.

### Rule 4: Shape Discount is Real Money at Scale
Choosing emerald over round saves ~17% at wholesale. That's $83 per stone at 1ct, but $203 per stone at 3ct. The savings are real — but emerald requires higher clarity to look good (inclusions visible through the open table).

### Rule 5: SI2 is Unmarketable at Large Sizes
A 5ct SI2 round lab-grown with a visible inclusion under the table is extremely hard to resell. Discount from VS1 is ~47% at 5ct and ~58% at 10ct — but there's almost no retail buyer for the large SI2 grade. Avoid SI2 above 3ct unless the inclusion is confirmed peripheral and eye-clean.

### Rule 6: The Alibaba Ceiling Test
For any white IGI lab-grown, the Wuzhou Messi Gems Alibaba price (D color, round) is the factory-floor ceiling for D color at that carat/clarity. A US jeweler buying G/VS1 should pay 15–25% above the Alibaba G-equivalent (D price ÷ 1.12 + importer friction). If asked to pay above the D/color Alibaba ceiling with no premium justification, walk away.

### Rule 7: Certificate Cost is Fixed, Not Proportional
An IGI certificate costs $85–$150 regardless of stone size. On a 0.5ct stone this is a meaningful cost (up to 30% of stone value). On a 5ct stone it is negligible (<5%). Factor this into your per-carat analysis at small sizes.

## Section 11: IGI Certificate Tips

- **Report number format:** LG + digits (lab-grown prefix) or plain digits. Always verify at `igi.org` before paying.
- **Inscription:** All IGI-certified stones should have the report number laser-inscribed on the girdle. Ask for the inscription to be visible in a macro photo or under loupe.
- **Growth method:** IGI states "Laboratory Grown" and the method (HPHT or CVD) on the cert. Ask which page shows it.
- **Treatment notation:** Look for "As Grown — No indication of post-growth treatment" vs. "High Pressure High Temperature treatment applied." HPHT treatment is common for CVD stones to fix gray color — not a defect per se, but documented.
- **IGI vs GIA:** GIA stopped grading lab-grown 4Cs in 2023. IGI is the current industry standard for lab-grown certification. A GIA grading report on a lab-grown stone is unusual and older.
- **Measurements matter:** Always request actual measurements (LxWxD in mm). Verify they match the cert. Out-of-spec measurements suggest the wrong stone.
