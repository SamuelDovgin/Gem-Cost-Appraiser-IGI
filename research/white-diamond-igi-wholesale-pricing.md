# White Lab-Grown Diamond — IGI Wholesale Pricing Guide

**Research Date:** May 2026 (v3.1 recalibration: May 20, 2026)  
**Scope:** IGI-certified, loose, lab-grown white/near-colorless diamonds.  
**Purpose:** Understand what a jeweler actually pays (their cost), not what they sell for.  
**Baseline shape:** Round Brilliant unless noted. All prices USD.  
**Baseline grade:** **E / VS1** (Alibaba commodity floor — not G/VS1).

See *jeweler-sourcing-ecosystem.md* → "Off-Catalog Grades" section for full buying strategy.

## Section 2: VVS1 vs VS1 Premium — Why It Scales with Carat

This is the **single most important insight** in the data:

| Carat | Messi VVS1/VS1 (D, round) | Estimated G-color VVS1/VS1 |
|-------|--------------------------|---------------------------|
| 0.50 ct | 1.24× (+24%) | 1.08× (+8%) |
| 1.00 ct | 1.06× (+6%) | 1.09× (+9%) |
| 1.50 ct | 1.38× (+38%) | 1.12× (+12%) |
| 2.00 ct | 1.98× (+98%) | 1.22× (+22%) |
| 3.00 ct | 2.14× (+114%) | 1.32× (+32%) |
| 4.00 ct | 1.59× (+59%) | 1.40× (+40%) |
| 5.00 ct | 1.49× (+49%) | 1.48× (+48%) |
| 6.00 ct | 1.40× (+40%) | 1.55× (+55%) |

**Why G-color premiums are less extreme than D at 2–3ct:** D+VVS1 at 3ct is a rarity multiplier effect (two hard-to-achieve grading factors combine). G/VVS1 at 3ct is more achievable, so the market premium is more moderate. However, at 5ct+ even G/VVS1 is genuinely hard to produce, so premiums converge.

**Rule:** A flat clarity multiplier (same % regardless of carat weight) is wrong. At 3ct, VVS1 costs ~32% more than VS1 (G color). At 10ct, VVS1 costs ~75% more than VS1. At 1ct the difference is only ~9%.

## Section 8: Price Rules Summary

### Rule 1: The Carat Non-Linearity
Lab-grown diamond price per carat is **NOT linear**. It falls at 2.5–3ct (oversupply pressure on the most common sizes) and then rises again at 4ct+ as production complexity increases. A 4ct stone is not simply 4× the 1ct price.

### Rule 2: Clarity Scales Hard — Even at 1ct (v3.1)
Do not use flat clarity multipliers. A VVS1 at 1ct is **+14%** over VS1. At 5ct it is **+52%**. At 10ct it is **+78%**. Below VS1 (VS2/SI) is off-catalog on most Alibaba IGI listings. **SI1 at 2ct is −56% below VS1** (not −15% as in old v3). SI2 at 2ct is −66%. These are distressed-lot prices, not standard catalog goods.

### Rule 3: Color — E is the Floor, Not G
Alibaba white IGI stock is **DEF + VVS/VS**. E/VS1 is the commodity baseline. D is +8% over E. **G is −12%** — not "basically the same." **H is −18% vs E**. I/J/K are −29%/−40%/−50% respectively — lab buyers can always source DEF stock, making H-Z off-catalog carry much steeper discounts than the legacy G-centered model implied.

### Rule 4: Shape Discount is Real Money at Scale
Choosing emerald over round saves ~17% at wholesale. That's $83 per stone at 1ct, but $203 per stone at 3ct. The savings are real — but emerald requires higher clarity to look good (inclusions visible through the open table).

### Rule 5: SI2 is Unmarketable at Large Sizes
A 5ct SI2 round lab-grown with a visible inclusion under the table is extremely hard to resell. Discount from VS1 is ~80% at 5ct and ~88% at 10ct — but there's almost no retail buyer for the large SI2 grade. Avoid SI2 above 3ct unless the inclusion is confirmed peripheral and eye-clean.

### Rule 6: The Alibaba Ceiling Test
For any white IGI lab-grown, the Wuzhou Messi Gems Alibaba price (D color, round) is the factory-floor ceiling for **D** at that carat/clarity. Convert to your color: **E ≈ D ÷ 1.08**, **G ≈ D ÷ 1.23**. A US jeweler buying E/VS1 should pay ~15–25% above the Alibaba E-equivalent. TikTok/direct at **$100 for ~1ct E/VS1** is at factory exit — not importer stock. If asked to pay far above the color-adjusted Messi ceiling with no beauty premium, walk away.

### Rule 7: Certificate Cost is Fixed, Not Proportional
An IGI certificate costs $85–$150 regardless of stone size. On a 0.5ct stone this is a meaningful cost (up to 30% of stone value). On a 5ct stone it is negligible (<5%). Factor this into your per-carat analysis at small sizes.

### Rule 8: Off-Catalog Grades Trade in the Secondary Market — Model Price is the Ceiling
VS2, SI1, SI2, and G-and-below color are **not standard Alibaba catalog items**. There is no factory price list to reference. These stones move in the jeweler-to-jeweler secondary market — backstock, liquidations, memo returns, estate lots. The model's `ws` price for these grades represents a **fair secondary market ceiling**, calibrated to what an informed buyer should pay. In practice:

- **Motivated sellers** (need cash fast, stone sitting 6+ months) will take 15–25% below model for SI1/SI2 and 10–20% below for lower-color VS1/VS2.
- **A VS2 in D/round is still Alibaba-listed** (Messi data shows VS2 ≈ VS1 price at 2ct for D). Secondary VS2 in G/H color is a double discount — off-catalog twice over.
- **SI1/SI2 have no Alibaba floor** to compete against. Model price is the only comp. Negotiate from there.
- **Do not pay model price as if it were a floor.** For off-catalog grades, model is the highest defensible price for an informed buyer.

## Section 11: IGI Certificate Tips

- **Report number format:** LG + digits (lab-grown prefix) or plain digits. Always verify at `igi.org` before paying.
- **Inscription:** All IGI-certified stones should have the report number laser-inscribed on the girdle. Ask for the inscription to be visible in a macro photo or under loupe.
- **Growth method:** IGI states "Laboratory Grown" and the method (HPHT or CVD) on the cert. Ask which page shows it.
- **Treatment notation:** Look for "As Grown — No indication of post-growth treatment" vs. "High Pressure High Temperature treatment applied." HPHT treatment is common for CVD stones to fix gray color — not a defect per se, but documented.
- **IGI vs GIA:** GIA stopped grading lab-grown 4Cs in 2023. IGI is the current industry standard for lab-grown certification. A GIA grading report on a lab-grown stone is unusual and older.
- **Measurements matter:** Always request actual measurements (LxWxD in mm). Verify they match the cert. Out-of-spec measurements suggest the wrong stone.
