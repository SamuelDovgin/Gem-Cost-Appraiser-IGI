# Diamond Cut & Shape Reference — Lab-Grown IGI

**Research Date:** May 2026  
**Scope:** All IGI-recognized cutting styles for lab-grown diamonds.  
**Baseline for pricing:** G / VS1 / Ideal / IGI at the indicated carat weight.  
**Price shown:** Estimated jeweler (wholesale) cost per stone.

---

## How to Read This Guide

Each entry covers:
- **Description:** What the cut looks like and how it performs
- **Facet count:** Approximate number of facets
- **Ideal proportions:** Table%, Depth%, Crown angle, Pavilion angle (where applicable)
- **L/W ratio:** Length-to-width range. "Sweet spot" = current market preference.
- **Wholesale multiplier vs Round:** How the cut prices vs round brilliant baseline
- **Clarity visibility:** How easily inclusions show in this cut
- **What to check:** Key things to verify before buying
- **Jeweler cost estimate:** G/VS1/Ideal/IGI at 1ct, 3ct, 5ct

Jeweler cost = Round G/VS1 × shape multiplier × carat multiplier (per [white-diamond-igi-wholesale-pricing.md](white-diamond-igi-wholesale-pricing.md)).

## 1. Round Brilliant

**Description:** The most researched and standardized diamond cut. 57 or 58 facets (with or without culet). Maximizes light return and brilliance. The benchmark all other cuts are measured against.

**Facet count:** 57 (no culet) or 58 (with culet)

**Ideal proportions (IGI Excellent):**
| Parameter | Ideal Range | Notes |
|-----------|-------------|-------|
| Table% | 53–58% | Smaller table = more fire |
| Depth% | 59.0–62.5% | Outside = leakage or fish-eye |
| Crown angle | 34.0–35.0° | |
| Pavilion angle | 40.6–41.0° | Critical for light return |
| Girdle | Thin to medium | Thick girdle adds carat, reduces face-up size |
| Culet | None to very small | Large culet shows as dark dot through table |
| Polish | Excellent | |
| Symmetry | Excellent | |

**L/W ratio:** 0.98–1.02. Ideal: 1.00. Round diamonds that are noticeably oval-shaped face-up are poorly cut.

**Wholesale multiplier:** 1.00 (baseline)

**Clarity visibility:** Moderate. Not a step cut, so inclusions are partly hidden by brilliance. However, table-centered inclusions and dark clouds are still visible in person.

**What to check:**
- Table% and depth% must both be in spec. Out-of-range depth% causes fish-eye (too shallow) or nail-head (too deep).
- Ask for Hearts & Arrows images for premium 3EX stones — confirms precise optical symmetry.
- Ask for ASET or IdealScope images to verify no leakage.
- Confirm no fluorescence issues (strong fluorescence can cause haziness in lab-grown CVD).

**Jeweler cost estimate (G/VS1/Ideal/IGI):**
| Carat | $/stone |
|-------|---------|
| 1ct | ~$91 |
| 2ct | ~$350 |
| 3ct | ~$489 |
| 5ct | ~$1,240 |
| 8ct | ~$3,040 |
| 10ct | ~$4,800 |

## 2. Oval Brilliant

**Description:** An elongated brilliant cut with the same fundamental facet layout as round. The elongated shape makes the stone appear larger face-up per carat than round. Subject to the "bowtie effect" — a dark bow-tie shaped shadow across the center — which ranges from invisible to severe.

**Facet count:** 57–58 (same pattern as round, stretched)

**Ideal proportions:**
| Parameter | Ideal Range |
|-----------|-------------|
| Table% | 53–63% |
| Depth% | 58–68% |
| L/W ratio | 1.35–1.45 (sweet spot 2026) |
| Girdle | Thin to slightly thick |

**L/W ratio guide:**
- < 1.25: Looks nearly round. Loses the "oval look." Discounted.
- 1.25–1.35: Shorter oval. Less popular. Slight discount.
- **1.35–1.45: Sweet spot. High demand 2026.**
- 1.45–1.55: Elongated. Acceptable, slightly less demand.
- > 1.55: Very elongated. Out of trend. Harder to sell.

**Wholesale multiplier:** 1.08 (+8% vs round)

**Clarity visibility:** Low-moderate. Brilliant faceting hides inclusions well. However, the bowtie area can reveal tilt-visible clouds.

**What to check:**
- Bowtie severity — ask for a slow tilt video in diffused light. A strong bowtie kills value.
- Whether the ends (points of the oval) go dark when tilted.
- Whether the belly of the oval looks full face-up — narrow bellies look like a squashed pear.
- Check L/W ratio from measurement certificate. Do not rely on photos which distort ratio.

**Jeweler cost estimate (G/VS1/Ideal/IGI):**
| Carat | $/stone |
|-------|---------|
| 1ct | ~$98 |
| 2ct | ~$378 |
| 3ct | ~$528 |
| 5ct | ~$1,339 |

## 3. Pear Modified Brilliant

**Description:** Teardrop shape. One rounded end and one pointed tip. Combines elements of round (the rounded end) and marquise (the pointed tip). Very elegant when properly proportioned; subject to bowtie and asymmetry issues.

**Facet count:** 56–58

**Ideal proportions:**
| Parameter | Ideal Range |
|-----------|-------------|
| Table% | 53–65% |
| Depth% | 58–66% |
| L/W ratio | 1.55–1.70 (sweet spot) |
| Shoulder symmetry | Must be even — check face-up |

**L/W ratio guide:**
- < 1.45: Squat pear. Looks stubby and unbalanced.
- 1.45–1.55: Acceptable; slightly short.
- **1.55–1.70: Sweet spot. Well-balanced point-to-belly.**
- 1.70–1.85: Long and elegant; slightly less in trend.
- > 1.85: Very elongated. Niche taste.

**Wholesale multiplier:** 1.05 (+5% vs round)

**Clarity visibility:** Low-moderate. Brilliant facets hide inclusions. However the pointed tip concentrates light differently — inclusions near the tip can show more.

**What to check:**
- Bowtie — can appear across the shoulder area.
- Whether both shoulders are even (draw a line across the widest point — it should bisect symmetrically).
- Whether the tip is sharp and protected (for setting purposes).
- Check for French-tip setting if point is sharp — reduces chipping risk.

**Jeweler cost estimate (G/VS1/Ideal/IGI):**
| Carat | $/stone |
|-------|---------|
| 1ct | ~$96 |
| 2ct | ~$368 |
| 3ct | ~$513 |
| 5ct | ~$1,302 |

## 3b. Moval (Marquise–Oval Hybrid)

**Description:** A hybrid silhouette between marquise and oval — elongated like both, with **softly rounded tips** instead of sharp marquise points. Marketed heavily in **lab-grown** jewelry in 2025–2026 but still a **non-standard** IGI cutting style name on many reports (may appear as modified oval or marquise).

**Facet count:** ~56–58 (brilliant family)

**L/W ratio:** **1.65–1.90** sweet spot (between pear and marquise)

**Wholesale multiplier:** **0.94 (−6% vs round)**

**Why discounted vs oval despite trendiness:** Oval commands a demand premium in lab wholesale (+8% in this model). Moval is more distinctive but **less standardized**, thinner resale liquidity, and often confused with oval/marquise on certs. Rapaport/retail guides place **marquise among the deepest fancy-shape discounts (~25%+ below round)**; moval sits **between marquise and oval** — better than marquise, not oval liquidity.

**Clarity visibility:** Similar to oval/marquise — bowtie risk; SI on elongated shapes is penalized in the appraiser.

**What to check:** Bowtie strength, tip symmetry, L/W not too squat or needle-like.

## 4. Marquise Brilliant

**Description:** Football-shaped (elongated, two pointed tips). One of the oldest fancy cuts — dating to 18th-century French court jewelry. Maximizes face-up size per carat: at the same carat weight a marquise shows approximately **10–15% more surface area** than a round, making it the strongest face-up-to-carat ratio among the pointed fancy shapes. Subject to bowtie. Tip protection critical for durability.

**Facet count:** 55–57

**Ideal proportions:**
| Parameter | Ideal Range |
|-----------|-------------|
| Table% | 53–65% |
| Depth% | 58–68% |
| L/W ratio | 1.90–2.10 (sweet spot) |

**L/W ratio guide:**
- < 1.75: Very wide/short. Stubby. Not classical marquise look.
- 1.75–1.90: Acceptable; chunkier.
- **1.90–2.10: Sweet spot. Classic marquise outline.**
- 2.10–2.30: Long. Elegant but harder to set; tip exposure risk increases.
- > 2.30: Very elongated. Niche; tip chips much more likely.

**Wholesale multiplier:** 0.87 (−13% vs round white) · 0.93 (−7% vs oval/pear for fancy color)

**Why discounted (white):** Lower demand relative to round, oval, pear. Tips are prone to chipping. Bowtie can be severe. Growing in popularity in 2025–2026 but still behind round/oval in liquidity. Natural-market benchmark: **Rapaport places marquise 20–25% below round at retail**; the lab-grown gap is narrower (~13%) because fashion trendiness and lower absolute price stakes both compress the penalty.

**Why smaller penalty for fancy color:** Marquise is a favored cut for **vivid pink and blue lab-grown**. Color concentrates toward the tips, visually deepening perceived saturation — cutters often orient marquise to exploit this effect. This drives higher relative demand in the fancy-color segment.

**2024–26 market context:** A broad **vintage/Art Deco revival** in bridal jewelry pushed marquise search volume and Alibaba listing depth upward meaningfully. Multiple celebrity engagements featuring marquise engagement rings accelerated the trend. Despite the resurgence, marquise still trails oval and pear in mainstream lab-grown resale liquidity.

**Clarity visibility:** Low-moderate. Brilliant faceting hides inclusions in the center and wings; **tip areas are more transparent** — inclusions or naturals near a tip are more visible. The bowtie region can also reveal tilt-visible clouds or feathers.

**Bowtie effect:** Marquise bowties are often **more pronounced than oval** — a dark bow-tie shadow runs perpendicular to the length axis. Severity ranges from faint/acceptable to dominant/value-killing. The **IGI cert does not grade bowtie**; it must be assessed in a slow tilt video under diffused lighting.

**Tip durability:** The two pointed tips concentrate mechanical stress; a hard knock can chip or crack an unprotected tip. **V-prong or full bezel/halo settings are strongly preferred** over standard prong heads. Add ~$50–200 to the setting budget vs a round/oval prong.

**Wing flatness:** Wings (curved sides) should be smoothly curved — flat or pinched wings signal a poorly cut stone and reduce face-up appeal.

**What to check:**
- Bowtie severity — request a slow tilt video in diffused light. Never rely on a static photo.
- Tip symmetry — both tips should align precisely on the long axis. Misaligned tips are a cut defect.
- Wing shape — wings should be gently curved, not flat, pinched, or bulging.
- Whether tips are protected in the proposed setting (V-prong or bezel recommended).
- L/W ratio from the measurement field on the cert — photos distort apparent ratio.
- For fancy color: check that color saturation appears even across the stone in face-up video; tips can look darker.
- Table% and depth% — out-of-range depth causes fish-eye or nail-head effects even in fancy shapes.

**Jeweler cost estimate (G/VS1/Ideal/IGI):**
| Carat | $/stone |
|-------|---------|
| 1ct | ~$79 |
| 2ct | ~$305 |
| 3ct | ~$426 |
| 5ct | ~$1,079 |

**Setting cost note:** V-prong or peg-head marquise heads are specialty items — not all benches stock them. Allow extra lead time and ~$50–200 over a standard round/oval prong setting.

## 5. Heart Modified Brilliant

**Description:** Heart-shaped brilliant. Symbolically significant but technically challenging to cut well. Two lobes, a cleft, and a point. Symmetry is paramount — asymmetric lobes or uneven cleft looks amateurish.

**Facet count:** 56–58

**Ideal proportions:**
| Parameter | Ideal Range |
|-----------|-------------|
| Table% | 53–65% |
| Depth% | 58–68% |
| L/W ratio | 0.95–1.05 |

**L/W ratio guide:**
- 0.95–1.05: Balanced heart. Both lobes even. Ideal.
- < 0.90: Very wide; lobes look flat and shallow.
- > 1.10: Narrow heart; looks like a stretched teardrop.

**Wholesale multiplier:** 0.86 (−14% vs round)

**Clarity visibility:** Low-moderate. Brilliance hides inclusions but open central area between lobes can show clouds.

**What to check:**
- Both lobes must be equal width and height — check face-up photo and video.
- Cleft must be sharp and well-defined.
- Point must be centered and sharp.
- Watch for dark center / bowtie effect in the indentation area.
- Best face-up in photos taken from directly above.

## 6. Cushion Brilliant / Cushion Modified Brilliant

**Description:** Square or rectangular shape with rounded corners. Two main variants: "chunky" (fewer, larger facets — patterned brilliance) and "crushed-ice" (many small facets — sparkly, scattered light). Very popular for fancy colored lab-grown diamonds. For white stones, both styles have strong demand.

**Facet count:** 56–64+ (varies by variant)

**Ideal proportions:**
| Parameter | Ideal Range |
|-----------|-------------|
| Table% | 57–67% |
| Depth% | 60–68% |
| L/W (square) | 1.00–1.05 |
| L/W (rectangular) | 1.10–1.30 |

**L/W ratio guide:**
- 1.00–1.05: Classic square cushion. Most popular white variant.
- 1.05–1.20: Slightly elongated. Transitional.
- 1.20–1.35: Rectangular cushion. Growing demand.
- > 1.40: Elongated cushion. More distinctive; less common.

**Wholesale multiplier:**
- Cushion Brilliant: 0.91 (−9% vs round)
- Cushion Modified Brilliant: 0.90 (−10% vs round)
- Square Cushion Modified: 0.90 (−10% vs round)

**Clarity visibility:** Low-moderate. Many facets scatter light, hiding inclusions well — especially crushed-ice variant. Chunky variant is slightly more transparent.

**What to check:**
- Determine if it's "chunky" or "crushed-ice" from video — buyer preference is personal but affects resale.
- Center black-out: Ask if the center goes dark/mushy when viewed face-up in normal light.
- Leakage from girdle area in very deep stones.
- Table windows in extremely shallow stones.

**Jeweler cost estimate (G/VS1/Ideal/IGI):**
| Carat | $/stone |
|-------|---------|
| 1ct | ~$82 |
| 2ct | ~$315 |
| 3ct | ~$440 |
| 5ct | ~$1,116 |

## 7. Cut Cornered Rectangular Modified Brilliant (Radiant)

**Description:** Rectangular shape with clipped corners. Combines brilliant-cut faceting with an emerald-cut outline. Very good light return. Less prone to windowing than step cuts. One of the most versatile fancy shapes.

**Facet count:** 62–70

**Ideal proportions:**
| Parameter | Ideal Range |
|-----------|-------------|
| Table% | 55–67% |
| Depth% | 60–70% |
| L/W ratio | 1.25–1.50 |

**L/W ratio guide:**
- 1.00–1.10: Very square. Use sq_radiant categorization.
- 1.10–1.25: Transitional. Less defined personality.
- **1.25–1.45: Sweet spot. Classic rectangular radiant.**
- 1.45–1.60: Elongated radiant. More "fancy" look; some demand.
- > 1.60: Very elongated. Niche.

**Wholesale multiplier:** 0.87 (−13% vs round)

**Clarity visibility:** Low. Brilliant faceting hides inclusions very effectively.

**What to check:**
- Whether facet pattern is "crushed-ice" (scattered) or more patterned. Personal preference.
- Center leakage: Some radiants have a watery/dark center — visible in face-up video.
- Corner darkness: Corners should remain bright. If they go dark, depth/proportions are off.
- Check the actual shape is more rectangular than square (confirm L/W from cert).

## 9. Princess Cut

**Description:** Square brilliant cut with pointed corners (no clipping). Very popular in the 2000s–2015; demand has declined as cushion and radiant gained share. Still common. Corners are the weakest structural point and MUST be protected in the setting (v-prongs or bezel).

**Facet count:** 58–76 (highly variable by cutter)

**Ideal proportions:**
| Parameter | Ideal Range |
|-----------|-------------|
| Table% | 60–75% |
| Depth% | 64–75% |
| L/W ratio | 1.00–1.05 |

**Wholesale multiplier:** 0.86 (−14% vs round)

**Clarity visibility:** Moderate. More transparent than cushion at the corners.

**What to check:**
- Corners must be intact — examine macro photos for chips.
- Whether setting will protect all four corners.
- Light return: large table% can cause large window under bright light.
- Ask about the chevron pattern count — 3–4 chevrons is typical excellent; more = more crushed.

## 10. Emerald Cut

**Description:** Rectangular step cut with clipped corners. Long, parallel facets arranged in steps rather than triangular brilliant facets. Maximizes color and clarity appearance — both good (beautiful color saturation) and bad (inclusions and windowing are very visible). Currently trending strongly in 2025–2026 for both natural and lab-grown.

**Facet count:** 50–58 (step-cut style — fewer, larger facets)

**Ideal proportions:**
| Parameter | Ideal Range |
|-----------|-------------|
| Table% | 60–72% |
| Depth% | 59–66% |
| L/W ratio | 1.35–1.55 |

**L/W ratio guide:**
- 1.20–1.35: Shorter emerald. Still attractive; chunkier.
- **1.35–1.50: Sweet spot. Classic emerald proportion.**
- 1.50–1.65: Elongated. Elegant; some demand.
- > 1.65: Very elongated. Niche; harder to set in standard settings.

**Wholesale multiplier:** 0.83 (−17% vs round)

**Why discounted despite being trending:**
- Step cuts require HIGHER clarity (VS1 minimum; VVS1 preferred). Cost of high-clarity stone offsets the shape discount.
- Less light return efficiency than brilliant cuts.
- Historically had surplus supply from Indian cutting houses.

**Clarity visibility:** HIGH. The open step-cut table is essentially a window into the stone. SI1 can be problematic; SI2 is almost always eye-visible. **Minimum recommended: VS2 for a clean look; VS1 preferred.**

**Clarity surcharge for emerald:** When buying emerald cut, the clarity premium from Section 5 is especially important because:
1. You MUST buy VS1 or better (add ~0–17% vs SI1)
2. At 3ct, VS1 emerald costs $406 — but you can't buy SI1 ($381) without visible inclusions

**What to check:**
- Window test: In face-up video, does the table look like a window (you can see through it)? This is the main defect in step cuts.
- Dark step effect: Are any of the steps extremely dark (black)? Some depth in steps is normal; complete darkness isn't.
- Inclusions: Ask for a high-magnification video at 10–20× showing the center of the stone.
- Corner condition: Emerald corners are beveled (step-cut), so chipping is less common than princess, but still check.

**Jeweler cost estimate (G/VS1/Ideal/IGI):**
| Carat | $/stone |
|-------|---------|
| 1ct | ~$76 |
| 2ct | ~$291 |
| 3ct | ~$406 |
| 5ct | ~$1,029 |

**Facet count:** 50–58

## 12. Trilliant Cut

**Description:** Triangular brilliant cut. Three sides, curved or straight. Used as both center stones and side stones. Very distinctive face-up shape. Less common as a center stone but growing in niche appeal.

**Facet count:** 43–50

**Ideal proportions:**
- Generally equilateral triangle — all sides equal
- Depth%: 32–48%
- Table%: 50–70%
- No standard L/W ratio — check that the triangle is equilateral

**Wholesale multiplier:** 0.82 (−18% vs round)

**Clarity visibility:** Moderate-High. The open triangular facets can window. VS2+ recommended.

**What to check:**
- All three corners must be protected in setting (usually v-prongs or bezel).
- All three sides should appear equal face-up.
- No windowing at the flat table area.
- If used as a side stone pair: both stones should match face-up area.

## 13. Old European Brilliant (OEB)

**Description:** Predecessor to the modern round brilliant. Larger facets, smaller table, higher crown, larger culet (often visible as a dark dot through the table). Cut by hand and eye before computerized proportioning. Warm, romantic light return — different "candle-flame" look vs modern round.

**Facet count:** 58 (same count, different proportions from modern round)

**Ideal proportions:**
- Table%: 40–55% (smaller than modern round)
- Depth%: 58–66%
- Crown height: Higher than modern round
- Culet: Small to medium (visible through table — characteristic feature)

**L/W ratio:** 0.98–1.02 (should still be round outline)

**Wholesale multiplier:** 0.92 (−8% vs round)

**Clarity visibility:** Low-moderate. The smaller table and larger facets actually hide inclusions better than modern cuts in some conditions.

**What to check:**
- The culet should be small and centered — a large culet shows as an obvious dark circle.
- In lab-grown, "old European" refers to the proportions — ask if it is genuinely OEB proportioned or just called that for marketing.
- Light performance is different from modern round: ask for video in candlelight or tungsten light to appreciate it.

**Jeweler cost estimate (G/VS1/Ideal/IGI):**
| Carat | $/stone |
|-------|---------|
| 1ct | ~$84 |
| 2ct | ~$322 |
| 3ct | ~$450 |

## 15. Rose Cut

**Description:** Flat base, domed top with triangular facets converging to a point. No pavilion. Very shallow stone — sits very flat in a setting. Does not return light like a brilliant cut. Antique/boho aesthetic; completely different visual from any other cut. Often translucent or slightly "sleepy" looking.

**Facet count:** 3–24 (simple rose) to 36 (full rose)

**Ideal proportions:**
- No standard depth% (dome height varies)
- Outline can be round, oval, pear, or free-form

**Wholesale multiplier:** 0.72 (−28% vs round)

**Clarity visibility:** LOW. The rose cut is actually quite forgiving of inclusions because the facet pattern and the translucent quality of the stone hide them well.

**What to check:**
- Whether the stone looks "sleepy" (hazy, not transparent). Some buyers like this; others don't.
- Whether the base is completely flat (for bezel or flush setting).
- The dome facet pattern — should be even and symmetrical from all angles.
- Color saturation in daylight — rose cuts show color beautifully.

**Jeweler cost estimate (G/VS1/IGI — note: "Ideal" cut grade doesn't formally apply):**
| Carat | $/stone |
|-------|---------|
| 1ct | ~$65 |
| 3ct | ~$352 |

## 16. Baguette Cut

**Description:** Long, narrow step cut. Rectangular, no clipped corners. Primarily used as side stones (channel-set alongside a center stone). Very simple geometric appearance. Extremely transparent — inclusions are clearly visible.

**Facet count:** 14 (very few)

**Ideal proportions:**
- Table%: 70–85%
- Depth%: 40–60%
- L/W ratio: 2.0–5.0+ (can be very elongated)

**Wholesale multiplier:** 0.76 (−24% vs round)

**Clarity visibility:** EXTREME. Only 14 facets with a very open table. VS1 is the minimum; VVS recommended. SI1+ will show obvious inclusions.

**What to check:**
- Any feathers or dark inclusions are very visible — ask for macro video.
- Corners are sharp (not clipped like emerald) — check for chips.
- If buying matched pairs, both stones must match in length, width, and color.
- Windowing is expected in baguettes — what matters is whether inclusions are visible.

**Jeweler cost estimate (G/VS1/IGI):**
| Carat | $/stone |
|-------|---------|
| 1ct | ~$69 |
| 2ct | ~$266 |

## 17. Tapered Baguette Cut

**Description:** Same as baguette but one end is narrower than the other — creates a taper. Used exclusively as side stones in pairs, flanking a center stone, to follow the taper of the ring shank.

**Facet count:** 14

**Wholesale multiplier:** 0.74 (−26% vs round) — priced below straight baguette because they must be bought in matched pairs and are harder to inventory.

**Clarity visibility:** EXTREME. Same as baguette.

**What to check:** Same as baguette, plus confirm the taper matches the intended setting. Tapered baguettes are made-to-order more often than straight baguettes.

## 18. Carre Cut

**Description:** Square step cut, similar to a square asscher but without clipped corners. Extremely geometric. Rarely used as a center stone; more common as an accent or design element.

**Facet count:** ~18–24

**L/W ratio:** 0.98–1.05

**Wholesale multiplier:** 0.80 (−20% vs round)

**Clarity visibility:** Very HIGH. Open step-cut table on a square shape.

---

## 18b. Hexagonal Dutch / Dutch Marquise

**Description:** Also called **Dutch Marquise** — an elongated **hexagonal** outline (six straight sides) instead of curved marquise wings and pointed tips. Custom / bespoke cutting; rare on mass Alibaba SKUs.

**Wholesale multiplier:** **0.82 (−18% vs round)**

**Why discounted:** Geometric marquise alternatives are **designer cuts** with very limited factory inventory and resale pools. Worse liquidity than standard marquise (already −13% vs round). Similar niche to hexagonal brilliant and Flanders.

**L/W ratio:** **1.85–2.10** (marquise-like elongation)

**What to check:** Symmetry of six sides, bowtie, tip protection in setting.

---

## 19. Portuguese Cut

**Description:** A brilliant cut with extra "cross" facets — 72 total facets. Creates a complex, fiery sparkle pattern with additional dispersed light compared to round brilliant. Deep stone (higher depth%) that can appear smaller face-up per carat than its weight suggests.

**Facet count:** 72

**Ideal proportions:**
- Table%: 50–58%
- Depth%: 65–75% (notably deep)
- L/W: 0.98–1.02

**Wholesale multiplier:** 0.85 (−15% vs round)

**Why discounted despite extra facets:** The extra depth% means the stone faces up smaller than a round of the same carat. Buyers notice; sellers must price lower. Also, it's an unusual cut with a smaller secondary market.

**What to check:**
- Compare face-up diameter to a round of the same carat — the Portuguese will be smaller due to depth.
- The additional facets should create even fire distribution, not create bright/dark patches.
- Confirm it is genuinely a Portuguese cut (ask for table/depth and facet diagram).

---

## 20. Flanders Cut

**Description:** Octagonal step-brilliant hybrid cut with 8 sides. Named after the Flemish gem-cutting tradition. Has both step and brilliant facets, creating a unique visual effect.

**Facet count:** ~61

**Wholesale multiplier:** 0.83 (−17% vs round)

**Clarity visibility:** Moderate. The hybrid faceting hides inclusions better than pure step cuts.

---

## 21. Half Moon Modified Brilliant

**Description:** Exactly half of a round brilliant, cut along the diameter. Used exclusively as side stones flanking a center stone. The flat edge is set against the girdle of the center stone.

**Facet count:** ~28–32

**L/W ratio:** 1.80–2.20 (measured along flat edge to curved arc)

**Wholesale multiplier:** 0.80 (−20% vs round)

**What to check:**
- If buying a pair: both must match in length (flat edge), width, depth, and color.
- The flat edge must be polished cleanly — any cracks or abrasions here are fatal for bezel setting.
- Windowing can occur in the center area.

---

## 22. Shield Modified Brilliant (Kite Cut)

**Description:** Shield or kite-shaped diamond. Triangular but with one elongated or pointed end, giving a kite or shield appearance. Very unusual; primarily an architectural or artistic cut.

**Facet count:** Variable (30–50)

**Wholesale multiplier:** 0.78 (−22% vs round)

**Note:** Rarely certified as "shield" — more often described as a modified brilliant of unusual shape. Price is highly variable based on face-up size and uniqueness.

---

## 23. Hexagonal Modified Brilliant

**Description:** Six-sided brilliant-cut diamond. Flat sides vs curved. Geometric, architectural appearance. Growing in bespoke/alternative jewelry.

**Facet count:** Variable

**Wholesale multiplier:** 0.79 (−21% vs round)

## Shape Selection Guide by Use Case

| Engagement ring center stone (maximum brilliance) | Round, Oval, Cushion | Highest light return; best resale |

| Alternative / bespoke | Shield, Hexagonal, Kite, Carre | Architectural; for designers |
